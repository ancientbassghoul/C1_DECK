## How to read this file

### [NTPvChat]
Need To Produce via ChatGPT image creation in a different conversation, and will later be named correctly and placed in the deck.
### [NTPvCode]
Need To Produce via Code locally. Image will be entered into the deck, either locally after downloading the deck, or by GPT, after uploading the image to the GPT project.
Final images will be called Raycast_Slide_#_Visual.png So if you see that in the project library while building the deck - you can use that.

IMPORTANT: The Speech bubble, and the mascot will appear as the slide's final overlay layer, after a user's click. It'll appear on top of whatever's already in the canvas, and the slide's layout should NOT take into account space for the speech bubble.
- **Speaker notes** — Guidance for presenting.

# Act II — By Hand, For Now

## Slide 7
**Title:** Ten Manual Graphics Overlay Masks
**Body:** `HUD_REGIONS`: 10 rectangles, geometrically masking every HUD overlay element in raw camera pixels. The top-centre navigation bar alone is split into **three** separate boxes — because one wide box, dragged through fisheye undistortion, would bow at the corners and expose exactly the content it was supposed to hide.
**Visual:** The undistorted frame with all 10 mask boxes drawn as colored overlays, one color per logical HUD element. [NTPvCode]

## Slide 8
**Title:** The Manual Correspondence Picker Is Born
**Body:**
- Multi-frame grid, independent zoom/pan, adjustable marker size
- Four feature types, cycled with a key: `ground`, `roof`, `wheel_axis`, `roof_edge` — the last two are *pairs* with known real-world distances (wheelbase, van width) baked in as hard constraints
- Right-click removes a single point; `d` deletes a whole correspondence or just one frame's mark, depending on context; `[`/`]` step through saved correspondences filtered by type
- A quit-safety net: unsaved changes require a second keypress to actually discard
**Visual:** A real screenshot of the grid UI mid-session — cyan/magenta/orange markers across several frame tiles. [NTPvCode + OBS]
**Speaker notes:** This is worth a real screenshot, not a mockup — it genuinely looks like a purpose-built desktop app, which surprises people expecting a debug script.

## Slide 9
**Title:** The Scorer: A Heads-Up, Not a Verdict
**Body:** For every correspondence, crop the clicked point in each frame, embed with CLIP, average pairwise similarity. A low score is genuinely ambiguous: it can mean *"wrong pick"* — or *"right pick, just doesn't look alike from this angle/blur."* CLIP only sees pixels, not the context that told you it's the same rivet.
**Visual:** Three crop pairs side by side, unlabeled at first glance: (1) high score, obviously the same feature; (2) low score, *still correct* — just an oblique-angle or blur mismatch; (3) low score, *actually* a mis-click. The point: (2) and (3) look equally "bad" to the algorithm. Only a human looking at the full frame — not just the crop — can tell them apart. [NTPvCode]
**Speaker notes:** Emphasize what it's *not*: not a 3D geometric check, purely "do these crops look alike" — and that's a real limitation, not a footnote. The project's own README says as much: low scores across oblique frame pairs are *expected*, not necessarily wrong.

## Slide 10
**Title:** "Manual Feature Matching Works Great on 7 Frames"
**Body:** Known van geometry (wheel axis, roof edges) as hard anchors + hand-picked ground correspondences → a genuinely good calibration on the 7 clearest frames. This becomes the quality bar the rest of the project measures itself against.
**Visual:** The best-looking proof-sheet reprojection from this era, one source pixel accurately landing across all 7 frames. [NTPvCode]
**Speech bubble:** *"This exact commit also complains, in its own message, about frame 12035 hitting the solver's yaw and roll limits, bit it was a small matter of releasing the limits in the config, and the hard frame got solved as well."*
**Speaker notes:** Good beat to pause on — first real proof of concept, worth letting it breathe before complicating it.

## Slide 11
**Title:** The Other Six
**Body:** Two-stage auto-matching: manual correspondences as a trusted anchor, LightGlue auto-matches extending coverage outward from there. First appearance of a pattern that gets reinvented twice more before this project is done: **trust a good core, extend outward carefully.**
**Visual:** A simple diagram — 7 solid "trusted" nodes in the center, 6 dashed "extended" nodes reaching out from them. [NTPvChat]
**Speech bubble:** "Yeah, so much effort, and great thinking, but it just didn't F***ing solve."

## Slide 19
**Title:** Heart Surgery
**Body:** Deleted outright: LightGlue, GroundingDINO, SAM, SuperPoint, the debug UI built for them. In: **MASt3R-SfM** (dense 3D reconstruction + camera poses) + **Qwen VL** (open-world object discovery) + **CLIP** (confidence weighting).

## Slide 20
**Title:** From Hard Constraints to Weighted Guesses
**Body:** Old approach: deterministic, hand-verified geometric constraints (manual clicks, known van dimensions) forced into a hard-constraint solve. New approach: every input — MASt3R points, the anchor object, GPS — is a **weighted, uncertain sensor reading**, fused probabilistically in Ceres. GPS is explicitly demoted to a post-hoc alignment step, never a solver constraint.
**Visual:** Two small diagrams side by side: "old" = rigid scaffolding holding up a structure; "new" = a weighted-average cloud of soft springs pulling toward consensus. [NTPvChat]
**Speaker notes:** This is the conceptual heart of the whole second half of the project — worth taking slow.

## Slide 20 - NEW
**Title:** Enter MASt3R
**Body:** The old stack just couldn't crack it, and something smarter was needed. MASt3R's dense reconstruction could handle the low-texture, repetitive ground the old feature matchers choked on.
**Visual:** Created.
**Speaker notes:** Mention that this magic requires better hardware.

## Slide 21
**Title:** Step Zero: Get a GPU
**Body:** RunPod, chosen over Google Colab specifically because Colab's runtimes wipe on timeout — meaning MASt3R's CUDA/C++ kernels would need recompiling from scratch every session, versus RunPod's persistent volume storage compiling once and staying ready.
**Visual:** A simple two-box comparison card, Colab vs. RunPod, with the "recompile every session" vs "compile once" distinction as the headline. [NTPvChat]
**Speaker notes:** Frame this as an actual evaluated decision, not a default — it's easy to skip over infrastructure choices in a deck, don't.

## Slide 22 -SKIP
**Title:** GPU Sniping, Host Timeouts, and a Very Expensive `pip install`
**Body:** Real friction, none of it in git: GPUs vanishing between page-load and click, host containers timing out mid-deploy, and — the costly one — dependencies installed onto the pod's *ephemeral* disk instead of the persistent volume, risking a repeat of a ~15-minute compile every single time the pod stopped. SSH + tmux, eventually, so a long-running session could survive a dropped connection instead of dying with it.
**Visual:** A wry "container disk vs. volume disk" diagram — one drawn as a sandcastle at the tideline, one as a filing cabinet. [NTPvChat]
**Speech bubble:** *"I saw your correspondence with Gemini, and I'm still shaking from the intensity of your hatred. Remind me not to piss you off like that, ever."*
**Speaker notes:** This is a good "human cost of infrastructure" slide — light on code, heavy on relatable pain. And for the record: what you landed on — persistent venv, models cached to a real directory, SSH+tmux — is exactly what the setup docs say to do today. Worth the pain.

## Slide 23
**Title:** Compute Headless in the Cloud, Test Locally
**Body:** RunPod has no display; the interactive viewer needs one. `--export-solve` runs the full pipeline remotely and serializes camera poses + terrain point clouds to disk. Those files travel to the local machine, where `--import-solve` skips OCR/MASt3R/Ceres entirely and opens the viewer straight against the already-solved cameras.
**Visual:** A literal "cloud → local" diagram: RunPod box producing `solved_cameras.json` + point-cloud files, an arrow labeled "copy down," a local laptop box opening the viewer. [NTPvChat]
**Speaker notes:** Close this slide on the line that sets up the next act: for most of this project, the *only* thing crossing that gap was a handful of numbers — the actual 3D reconstruction never left the pod. That's about to matter.

# Act V — How the New Brain Thinks

## Slide 23.5
**Title:** MASt3R's Achilles' heel
**Body:** MASt3R has one weakness - it DRIFTS. We will use QWEN to find a joint anchor in the images, to prevent this.

## Slide 24

**Body:** Per frame: list every man-made object, bbox + label. Across all frames: consolidate labels, rank by how many frames contain each one. ## Slide 27
**Title:** The Anchor Bbox Bug
**Body:** Qwen2.5-VL's bbox format was assumed to be `[ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax]` in a normalized `[0,1000]` space. It's actually `[xmin, ymin, xmax, ymax]` in **absolute pixels** of Qwen's own internally-resized image. Every anchor centroid, in every one of 13 frames, landed in the wrong place — until `--preview-anchor`'s saved images made it undeniable.
**Visual:** A real (or recreated) pair of `--preview-anchor` output images: one with the bbox scattered off the van, one with it correctly boxing the van after the fix — same frame, before/after. [NTPvCode]
**📸 Screenshot/recording opportunity:** the *fixed* state is reproducible right now via `--preview-anchor`; the *broken* state would need checking out the commit just before `ca13cf6` to regenerate faithfully — worth doing once for the "before" half of this slide, it's a strong visual.
**Speaker notes:** This is the single best "show the actual bug" slide in the deck — don't paraphrase it, show the picture.

## Slide 28
**Title:** Describing a Symbol an AI Can't Ground
**Body:** Qwen was asked to find "a + or ⊕ symbol" for the crosshair reticle. The real on-screen icon: a small ring of four chevron marks around a center dot — nothing like a plus sign. Qwen, unable to ground the wrong description, defaulted to guessing near the frame's center — landing ~190px off on one verified frame.
**Visual:** Side-by-side: what was described (a big fat "+") vs. what's actually on screen (the chevron-ring reticle), with the wrong guessed location circled. [NTPvChat]
**Speaker notes:** Good light, honestly funny beat — worth a small pause for a laugh before moving on.

## Slide 29
**Title:** A Bar Set for a Different Kind of Dataset
**Body:** `CLIP_ANCHOR_THRESHOLD` started at 0.85 — the exact cosine-similarity cutoff the June 8th feedback suggested (Slide 15), a solid bar for a typical, well-lit dataset. But real scores on this specific bespoke footage — blurry, repetitive, low-texture agricultural ground — never came close (they run 0.15–0.25), silently zeroing out every anchor-ray constraint in the solver until someone looked at the real distribution and lowered the bar to match.
**Visual:** A simple number line: the suggested threshold marked toward the right, the entire real data cluster sitting well to its left, labeled not "wrong" but "this dataset's actual range." [NTPvChat]
**Speaker notes:** First appearance of a config value ruining the result. Will happen again with MASt3R's Confidence Gate — plant it here, pay it off then. # Act VII — The Case of the Silent Failures

## Slide 30
**Title:** No Exception. No Warning. Just… Nothing.
**Body:**
```python
raw_poses = scene.im_poses if hasattr(scene, "im_poses") else []
```
`SparseGA` (MASt3R's actual result object) has no `.im_poses` attribute — that belongs to a *different* class, `dust3r`'s dense aligner. `hasattr` quietly returns `False`. `raw_poses` is silently `[]`. Every camera pose extraction returns nothing. The solver aborts before Ceres ever runs. The whole pipeline falls back to raw telemetry — marked only by a single log line, forever, until someone reads the logs closely.
**Visual:** The three-line code snippet, large, on an otherwise near-empty slide. Let it be the whole slide. [NTPvCode]
**Speech bubble:** These kind of bugs gave birth to the very popular rule in the claude.md file of the next project: NEVER code silent fallbacks to anything. If something is missing - shout about it loudly.
**Speaker notes:** Read the code out loud if presenting live. It's more effective than any diagram — the bug is legitimately that small and that quiet.

## Slide 31
**Title:** Right Numbers, Wrong Matrix
**Body:** `solved_cameras.json` stores heading/pitch/roll, not the raw rotation matrix. Rebuilding it locally used a generic library assumption (three fixed-axis rotations multiplied together) — but this project's actual rotation math applies roll as a Rodrigues rotation *around the camera's own forward axis*, not a fixed world axis. Every camera reloaded via `--import-solve` was silently using an unrelated orientation.
**Visual:** Two rotation-matrix "fingerprints" (small colored grids) side by side, visibly different, captioned "same three angles, two completely different matrices." [NTPvChat]
**Speaker notes:** Pair this conceptually with slide 27 — two bugs, same shape: nothing crashes, everything's just quietly wrong.

## Slide 32 — Fixing blindness, not just bugs
**Title:** `--export-mesh`: Actually Looking
**Body:** Born in the exact same commit as both silent-failure fixes above: a debug tool that dumps the raw MASt3R reconstruction (and, once solved, the Ceres-refined + Sim(3)-aligned version) as real `.glb` scenes, importable straight into Blender.
**Visual:** A real Blender screenshot of the point cloud + camera cards, if available; otherwise a simple render mockup of scattered 3D points with camera frustums. [NTPvCode]
**Speaker notes:** The moral, stated plainly: two silent bugs in a row is what it took to justify building a tool whose whole job is "let a human actually look at the 3D result." Worth saying out loud.

# Act VIII — Tuning the Machine

## Slide 33
**Title:** Attention Magnets
**Body:** Hard black HUD fills acted as infinite-contrast "attention magnets" for MASt3R's vision transformer, warping its initial pose estimates. Fixed with a feathered gray fill — Gaussian-blurred, no hard edge.
**Visual:** Before/after crop of a masked HUD region: hard black rectangle vs. soft gray-blurred patch, same location. [NTPvCode]
**Speech bubble:** *"Nice fix. Don't get comfortable — it comes with a price. You'll see it next slide."*
**Speaker notes:** A genuinely nice, non-obvious ML-adjacent finding — worth a beat of its own.

## Slide 34
**Title:** The Gray Fill's Side Effect
**Body:** The softer fill quietly depressed MASt3R's confidence scores *scene-wide*, not just where it was applied. A single fixed confidence cutoff then wiped out nearly every pixel in 11 of 13 frames. Fix: a **per-frame adaptive gate** — each frame's own threshold, floor-protected, with a guaranteed top-256-pixel fallback so no frame is ever wiped to zero. Diagnostic logging added so the next scale shift is visible immediately instead of silent.
**Visual:** A small bar chart: "pixels kept per frame" before (mostly empty bars) vs. after (consistently populated bars). [NTPvChat]
**Speaker notes:** Worth noting quietly, not making a big deal of: the diagnostic logging shipped in the same commit as the fix this time, rather than only after another silent failure forced the issue.

## Slide 35
**Title:** When "Good Enough" Depends on Scale
**Body:** A 3D-distance track-quality gate, measured in MASt3R's own arbitrary units, let bad tracks through whenever the eventual real-world scale factor happened to be large — observed as high as ~18.8×. Replaced with a **scale-invariant** check: does this point, projected back into its own source frame, land within 5 pixels of where it was actually seen?
**Visual:** A "before" scattered/noisy point cloud vs. "after" clean point cloud, same frame set. [NTPvCode]
**Speaker notes:** Nice concrete example of "the right invariant" fixing a whole class of bug at once.

## Slide 36
**Title:** Flat Earthers...
**Body:** Replaced the flat `z = 0` ground-plane assumption with an interpolated height field built from the solved 3D points themselves — ray-casting now iterates to find where a ray actually meets the real, bumpy terrain, falling back to flat only if that doesn't converge.
**Visual:** A flat plane's wireframe. On top of it, terrian wireframe, a few rays hit the terrian, converge on one point. The continuation of the rays hit the flat plane at various points on the plane. [NTPvChat]
**Speaker notes:** At that stage, things started looking good. But I wanted to try to do better.

# Act IX — The Twist

## Slide 37
**Title:** Two Final Commits
**Body:** An incremental two-stage Ceres solve (lock down the good frames first, register the blurry ones motion-only against that structure). Then, one level deeper: splitting MASt3R's *own* reconstruction the same way — a clean backbone built only from good frames, with each bad frame individually resectioned against it so it can never contaminate another bad frame or the backbone itself. Both are the "trust a good core, extend outward" pattern from Act II — reinvented a third time, one layer deeper than ever before.
**Visual:** The two-stage MASt3R diagram — a tight, clean cluster labeled "backbone," with individual bad frames shown resectioning in one at a time from the outside, each with its own dotted connector. [NTPvChat]
**Speaker notes:** Let this slide feel like a triumph on its own terms — the reasoning genuinely is sound. The next slide is the gut-punch, don't preview it here.

## Slide 38
**Title:** Quit while you're ahead
**Body:** Despite the reasoning behind both final commits being sound, **neither produced better real-world output.** The project's best empirically-observed reprojection quality was three commits earlier. No crash. No silent-empty-result pattern. Just: worse, in practice, for reasons the commits' own diagnostics never caught.
**Visual:** An image of the point cloud generated by the last version of the code. [NTPvCode]
**Speaker notes:** Worth mentioning that this whole effort was across half a day. As feedback was already good, we decided to move on to the next challenge, knowing we can continue fighting it another day.
